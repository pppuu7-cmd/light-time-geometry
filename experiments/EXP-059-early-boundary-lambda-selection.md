# EXP-059 — Early-boundary wavefunction and finite-Lambda state-selection test

## Objective

EXP-058 showed:

\[
\text{quantization}
\neq
\text{state selection}.
\]

Now test the active proposal:

\[
\mathcal A(n\mid\Psi_{\rm early})
\]

or equivalently a distribution over the cosmological constant generated by an early global boundary state.

Use the unimodular/global-conjugacy structure as the minimal model.

The decisive question is:

> Can a simple early state with no late cosmological scale encoded in it produce a sharply preferred finite positive \(\Lambda_*\)?

---

## 1. Normalize the conjugate pair

Write the cosmological action term schematically as

\[
I_\Lambda=-P_\Lambda\,T,
\]

where

\[
P_\Lambda
\equiv
\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}
\]

and

\[
T\equiv V_4
\]

up to convention-dependent normalization.

Then the unimodular quantum structure treats these as conjugate global variables:

\[
\boxed{
[\hat P_\Lambda,\hat T]
=
i\hbar.
}
\]

Therefore the \(P_\Lambda\)-representation and the four-volume-time representation are related by a Fourier transform.

---

## 2. Minimal early Gaussian state

Take the simplest early state localized in global four-volume time with no conjugate phase:

\[
\Psi_{\rm early}(T)
=
\mathcal N
\exp\left[
-\frac{T^2}{4\sigma_T^2}
\right].
\]

Its Fourier transform is

\[
\widetilde\Psi(P_\Lambda)
\propto
\exp\left[
-\frac{\sigma_T^2P_\Lambda^2}{\hbar^2}
\right].
\]

Therefore the probability density is peaked at

\[
\boxed{
P_\Lambda=0
}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda=0.
}
\]

Its width is of order

\[
\Delta P_\Lambda
\sim
\frac{\hbar}{\sigma_T}.
\]

### Result 059A

A featureless zero-phase early wavepacket does not select a finite positive cosmological constant.

It selects zero as its natural central value, with a width fixed by the inverse four-volume uncertainty.

---

## 3. How to shift the peak to finite Lambda

Now multiply the early state by a phase,

\[
\Psi_{\rm early}(T)
=
\mathcal N
\exp\left[
-\frac{T^2}{4\sigma_T^2}
\right]
\exp\left[
\frac{iP_*T}{\hbar}
\right].
\]

Its Fourier transform is shifted:

\[
\widetilde\Psi(P_\Lambda)
\propto
\exp\left[
-\frac{\sigma_T^2(P_\Lambda-P_*)^2}{\hbar^2}
\right].
\]

Therefore the late distribution is peaked at

\[
\boxed{
P_\Lambda=P_*.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\Lambda_* = 8\pi G P_*.
\]

But the desired late cosmological scale has simply been inserted as the early conjugate phase.

### Result 059B

\[
\boxed{
\text{a finite peak can be generated,
but only if the early state already carries the corresponding phase scale.}
}
\]

This is state encoding, not scale emergence.

---

## 4. Width versus mean

Four-volume conjugacy naturally links the width of the cosmological-constant distribution to the duration/volume uncertainty:

\[
\Delta P_\Lambda\,\Delta T
\gtrsim
\frac{\hbar}{2}.
\]

This can make a late cosmological constant sharply defined when the conjugate global volume/time becomes enormous.

But it does not determine the mean:

\[
\langle P_\Lambda\rangle.
\]

This exactly matches the conclusion of EXP-055.

### Result 059C

The conjugate structure can explain:

\[
\boxed{
\text{sharpness of a selected Lambda}
}
\]

more naturally than:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the value of Lambda itself}.
}
\]

---

## 5. General lesson beyond the Gaussian benchmark

The Gaussian calculation is a minimal benchmark, not a theorem that every early state peaks at zero.

A more structured early wavefunction can produce:

- multiple peaks;
- a finite positive peak;
- oscillatory selection;
- suppressed large or small \(\Lambda\).

But every such feature must come from:

1. structure in the boundary wavefunction;
2. a nontrivial transition kernel;
3. topology/flux data;
4. matter dynamics;
5. a global constraint.

Thus a finite \(\Lambda_*\) can certainly emerge from **dynamics**, but not from the bare conjugacy relation alone.

## 6. Translation to the conditional horizon level n

EXP-057 gives, conditionally,

\[
H_n^2
=
\frac{c^5}{2G\hbar n}.
\]

A late distribution over \(\Lambda\) is therefore equivalent to a distribution over \(n\).

A featureless conjugate-state construction does not prefer a particular finite huge \(n\).

A finite peak

\[
n_*
\]

requires an early phase, transition kernel, or measure with an independent dimensionless hierarchy.

So the state-selection problem has not disappeared under the discrete description.

## 7. Dimensional-analysis form of the hierarchy problem

If the early theory contains only

\[
G,\hbar,c
\]

and order-unity dimensionless data, its natural curvature scale is Planckian.

A late state with

\[
n\gg1
\]

requires a mechanism that generates a huge dimensionless number.

Possible mechanisms can include:

- exponential dimensional transmutation;
- a large topological/winding integer;
- a large number of degrees of freedom;
- long dynamical evolution producing an invariant count;
- a nonperturbative saddle action.

But one of these must be derived.

### Result 059D

\[
\boxed{
\text{a huge late hierarchy requires a huge dimensionless invariant or a mechanism that generates one.}
}
\]

It cannot be obtained by dimensional analysis from \(G,\hbar,c\) alone.
