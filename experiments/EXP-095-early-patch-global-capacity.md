# EXP-095 — Early Planck patch versus global capacity: where can the future states be?

## Objective

Combine EXP-093 and EXP-094.

Ask whether the early Planck-scale causal patch can itself contain the entire Hilbert-space capacity needed for a huge late de Sitter horizon.

---

## 1. Early accessible LTG capacity

At the Planck Hubble scale,

\[
H_i=H_P,
\]

EXP-078 gives

\[
\mathcal N_i=\frac12.
\]

The associated Bekenstein-Hawking entropy is

\[
\frac{S_i}{k_B}
=
2\pi\mathcal N_i
=
\pi.
\]

Thus the semiclassical accessible-state count is formally

\[
D_i^{\rm acc}
\sim
e^\pi.
\]

This is only order \(10^1\) in state-count language.

---

## 2. Late de Sitter capacity

For a late de Sitter horizon,

\[
\frac{S_*}{k_B}
=
\ln D_{\rm tot}
\]

in the finite-capacity interpretation.

For the observed-scale benchmark,

\[
S_*/k_B
\sim
10^{122}.
\]

Therefore

\[
D_{\rm tot}
\sim
\exp(10^{122}),
\]

enormously larger than the early accessible count.

---

## 3. Unitary implication

If the early Planck causal patch were literally the entire closed Hilbert space, then unitarity would forbid its dimension from later becoming

\[
D_{\rm tot}\gg D_i^{\rm acc}.
\]

Therefore at least one of the following must be true:

1. the early causal patch was only a small subsystem/code sector of a much larger total state space;
2. the fundamental theory does not use a fixed finite-dimensional Hilbert space;
3. the simple horizon-entropy-as-exact-dimension interpretation fails at the earliest regime;
4. the cosmological description requires degrees of freedom not represented by the early semiclassical horizon area.

### Result 095A

\[
\boxed{
\text{early small horizon capacity}
\ne
\text{total fundamental capacity}
}
\]

in any fixed-Hilbert-space unitary model that later contains a huge de Sitter entropy.
