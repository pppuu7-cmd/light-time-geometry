# EXP-032 — Tangent/cotangent dual-norm closure

## Question

Can the intuition "light and time are two sides of one structure; mass and geometry complete the picture" be made precise without simply renaming standard relativity?

## 1. Coordinate-side identity

For a local inertial frame and a timelike trajectory,

\[
c^2d\tau^2=c^2dt^2-d\ell^2.
\]

Define

\[
\beta_x\equiv\frac{d\ell}{c\,dt},
\qquad
\alpha_\tau\equiv\frac{d\tau}{dt}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\beta_x^2+\alpha_\tau^2=1.}
\]

The endpoints are

\[
\text{rest: }(\beta_x,\alpha_\tau)=(0,1),
\]

and the null limit

\[
\text{light: }(\beta_x,\alpha_\tau)=(1,0).
\]

This is standard SR.

## 2. Momentum-side identity

The mass shell is

\[
E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4.
\]

Define

\[
\beta_p\equiv\frac{pc}{E},
\qquad
\alpha_m\equiv\frac{mc^2}{E}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\beta_p^2+\alpha_m^2=1.}
\]

For an on-shell massive particle,

\[
\boxed{\beta_p=\beta_x,\qquad \alpha_m=\alpha_\tau.}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\tau}{dt}=\frac{mc^2}{E}
}
\]

is the exact bridge between the proper-time fraction and the normalized rest-energy fraction.

## 3. Covariant formulation

Coordinate displacement lives in the tangent space:

\[
X^2\equiv g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu.
\]

Momentum lives in the cotangent space:

\[
P^2\equiv g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu.
\]

For the `(-,+,+,+)` convention,

\[
X^2=-c^2d\tau^2,
\qquad
P^2=-m^2c^2.
\]

Therefore proper time and mass are the magnitudes of the timelike norm on dual spaces generated by the same metric/inverse metric pair.

This yields the structural diagram

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\text{tangent space} & \xleftrightarrow{\;g\;} & \text{cotangent space}\\
 c\,d\tau & & m c\\
 d\ell/(c dt) & & pc/E\\
 \text{null cone} & & \text{massless shell}
\end{array}
\]

The diagram is conceptually useful but all entries are standard differential geometry and relativistic mechanics.

## 4. Action pairing

For a free massive particle,

\[
p_\mu=m u_\mu,
\qquad
u^\mu \text{ is not used; } u^\mu\equiv\frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau}.
\]

Then

\[
p_\mu dx^\mu=-mc^2d\tau,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
S=\int p_\mu dx^\mu=-mc^2\int d\tau.
}
\]

The quantum phase is

\[
\boxed{
\Phi=\frac{S}{\hbar}
=-\frac{mc^2}{\hbar}\int d\tau.
}
\]

This is the invariant object that multiplies mass and proper time.

## 5. Is light the "minus" of time?

No, not literally.

In the Lorentzian metric the sign opposition is between the temporal coordinate contribution and spatial coordinate contribution:

\[
-c^2dt^2+d\ell^2.
\]

Light is the **zero of the resulting norm**:

\[
-c^2dt^2+d\ell^2=0.
\]

Therefore the strongest mathematically accurate reformulation is:

> light is the null-balance state of the same geometry in which massive worldlines acquire a nonzero proper-time norm.

The "opposite side" to spatial propagation inside the normalized timelike identity is proper-time accumulation, not light as an independent signed quantity.

## 6. Where mass enters

The same geometry gives

\[
-g^{-1}(p,p)=m^2c^2.
\]

Thus a nonzero rest mass is the momentum-space counterpart of a nonzero proper-time norm.

This gives the exact pair

\[
\boxed{
\text{proper-time norm} \leftrightarrow \text{mass norm}.
}
\]

The massless/null boundary is

\[
\boxed{
d\tau=0,\qquad m=0}
\]

for an on-shell freely propagating massless particle.

## 7. Geometry does not require mass

A crucial falsification of the strongest emergence claim is already available inside standard GR:

- Minkowski spacetime has geometry even with `T_{mu nu}=0`;
- vacuum Einstein equations admit nontrivial curved solutions;
- gravitational waves propagate through vacuum;
- radiation has stress-energy and gravitates although its quanta are massless.

Therefore the proposition

\[
\text{mass appears} \Rightarrow \text{geometry appears}
\]

is false if "geometry" means existence of a spacetime metric.

A weaker proposition remains testable:

\[
\text{massive/timelike content changes the geometry differently from null content.}
\]

That statement is standard GR through the full stress tensor and, in particular, through differences in stress-energy trace and pressure.

## 8. Candidate four-part closure

The cleanest current LTG organization is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{rcl}
\text{geometry }g &:& \text{defines both causal and momentum norms},\\
\text{space} &:& \text{spatial component of tangent displacement},\\
\text{light} &:& \text{null boundary }g(dx,dx)=0,\\
\text{proper time} &:& \sqrt{-g(dx,dx)}/c,\\
\text{mass} &:& \sqrt{-g^{-1}(p,p)}/c.
\end{array}}
\]

The same metric therefore organizes all four notions without requiring them to be independent substances.

## 9. Novelty test: invariant algebra

At one spacetime point, with only a metric `g`, one vector `dx`, and one covector `p`, the obvious scalar invariants are generated by

\[
A=g(dx,dx),
\qquad
B=g^{-1}(p,p),
\qquad
C=p(dx).
\]

For an on-shell free massive trajectory,

\[
A=-c^2d\tau^2,
\qquad
B=-m^2c^2,
\qquad
C=-mc^2d\tau,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{C^2=AB}
\]

(up to the metric-sign convention already included in `A,B`).

This equality is the saturation condition that momentum and four-velocity are parallel. It introduces no new degree of freedom.

### EXP-032A result

The proposed time-light-mass-geometry closure exists exactly as a **dual Lorentz-norm structure**, but at lowest local order it closes on standard relativity.

### EXP-032B result

No fourth independent scalar appears from `{g, dx, p}` alone. A genuinely new LTG theory must add either:

- an additional physical field/structure;
- nonlocal/topological data;
- a new dynamical relation between stress-energy production and geometry;
- or a new observable constraint relating existing sectors.

The next branch should test whether the dual norm can become dynamical without an arbitrary extra field.

A particularly sharp question is:

> During a physical process that converts null quanta into massive particles, is there any invariant relation between the increase of the timelike momentum norm `m^2 c^2`, the accumulated proper-time phase, and a change in a geometric scalar or volume element that is stronger than Einstein + standard QFT already predicts?

This can be attacked using explicit pair-production stress tensors and local geometric response rather than new kinematic notation.
