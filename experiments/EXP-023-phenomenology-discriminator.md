# EXP-023 — Phenomenology discriminator for hidden-momentum mass

## Objective

Ask the most important experimental question reached so far:

> If 4D rest mass is hidden momentum, how could an observer distinguish that origin from an ordinary 4D mass term/Higgs-generated mass?

The answer must be based on observables, not interpretation.

## 1. Minimal single-circle KK prediction

With a compact circle and the full KK gauge connection,

\[
p_\psi=\frac{n\hbar}{R_0},
\]

and the 4D effective mass is

\[
\boxed{
m_n=\frac{|n|\hbar}{cbR_0}.}
\]

The same hidden momentum acts as a 4D gauge charge,

\[
\boxed{q_n=\kappa p_\psi
=\kappa\frac{n\hbar}{R_0}.}
\]

Therefore, if **all** of the observed rest mass and the observed `U(1)` charge came from the same hidden momentum,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{m_n}{|q_n|}
=\frac{1}{|\kappa|cb}
}
\]

would be universal for all nonzero modes at fixed radion `b`.

## 2. Immediate charged-lepton test

The electron and muon carry the same magnitude of electric charge, but their measured masses are very different:

\[
m_e\simeq0.511\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2,
\]

\[
m_\mu\simeq105.658\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2.
\]

Their mass ratio is about `206.8` even though the electric-charge magnitude is the same.

If the electromagnetic charge were simply

\[
q\propto p_\psi
\]

and the complete rest mass were simultaneously

\[
m\propto|p_\psi|,
\]

then equal `|q|` would require equal `|p_psi|` and therefore equal mass.

That is not observed.

### F-023A

**The minimal one-circle identification in which the same single hidden momentum generates both the entire Standard-Model electric charge and the entire rest mass is falsified by the charged-lepton spectrum.**

This does not falsify Kaluza–Klein theory generally. It falsifies only the simplest attempt to identify observed Standard-Model mass and electric charge with one and the same KK momentum.

## 3. KK tower test without electromagnetism

Suppose the off-diagonal KK gauge field is absent, irrelevant, or belongs to a hidden `U(1)`, so the charge objection is avoided.

The compact momentum still predicts a tower

\[
\boxed{m_n=|n|m_1}
\]

for a massless 5D parent field in the simplest fixed-radius circle compactification.

Thus a pure hidden-momentum origin predicts equally spaced masses in `|n|` before additional interactions/boundary terms are included.

The observed Standard-Model fermion mass spectrum is not a simple universal integer KK tower.

### F-023B

**A single free massless 5D field on a simple circle cannot reproduce the observed Standard-Model mass spectrum using KK momentum alone.**

Again, standard extra-dimensional model building can evade this with multiple fields, bulk masses, warping, boundary conditions, localization, Yukawa couplings, or other structure—but then hidden momentum is no longer the sole explanation.

## 4. Proper-time phase is not a discriminator by itself

For any effective 4D particle with rest mass `m`, regardless of whether `m` arose from

- a Higgs/Yukawa term;
- a bare effective mass;
- compositeness/binding energy;
- KK hidden momentum;
- another UV mechanism;

the low-energy worldline phase contains

\[
\boxed{
\Phi=\frac1\hbar\int mc^2d\tau.
}
\]

Therefore observing this phase alone cannot reveal the microscopic origin of `m`.

### F-023C

**The frame-invariant mass–proper-time phase found in LTG is robust but not source-discriminating.**

This is a crucial negative result: an atom-interferometer or clock experiment sensitive only to the standard relativistic action phase cannot distinguish hidden-momentum mass from an ordinary effective rest mass.

## 5. What would distinguish hidden-momentum mass?

A genuine hidden-momentum origin requires at least one additional observable beyond the ordinary mass shell and proper-time phase.

Natural discriminators are:

### A. Additional KK excitations

A compact direction predicts additional states with related hidden momentum numbers.

### B. Selection rules

Translation symmetry in the compact direction gives KK-number conservation or a remnant parity, producing characteristic pair-production/decay rules.

### C. Radion coupling

Because

\[
m_n\propto b^{-1}
\]

in the inherited frame, the field controlling the compactification radius couples to the mass sector. In the Einstein frame the mass dependence is transferred into an explicit scalar coupling.

A light unstabilized radion therefore produces fifth-force / equivalence-principle / varying-coupling signatures.

### D. Gauge charge correlation

If the KK vector is identified with a physical `U(1)`, the hidden momentum also fixes charge.

### E. Modified high-energy spectrum

At energies high enough to excite the compact dimension, scattering amplitudes must resolve the tower rather than only the single 4D effective mass.

## 6. Decoupling theorem for the interpretation

Suppose a model is engineered so that:

- all excited KK states are inaccessible;
- the radion is stabilized and decoupled;
- the KK gauge field is absent/hidden/decoupled;
- no residual selection rule is observable;
- the only low-energy remnant is one particle with fixed mass `m`.

Then its low-energy propagation is described by the same effective 4D mass shell and proper-time phase as an ordinary massive particle.

Operationally,

\[
\boxed{
\text{hidden-momentum origin}
\longrightarrow
\text{ordinary 4D mass parameter}
}
\]

once all companion degrees of freedom are decoupled.

### F-023D

**If every extra-dimensional companion observable is decoupled, the mass-origin interpretation is not experimentally distinguishable at low energy.**

Thus a distinct LTG theory cannot hide all of its extra structure and still claim the ordinary mass phase as a unique prediction.

## 7. Consequence for Standard-Model applications

The minimal LTG/KK null lift should not currently be interpreted as an alternative derivation of the known Standard-Model particle masses.

At most it provides:

1. a geometric toy mechanism for how a lower-dimensional mass can emerge from a higher-dimensional massless field;
2. a possible mechanism for a new/dark sector;
3. a language for classifying mass/proper-time phases and hidden stress.

To address actual Standard-Model masses, one would need additional structure and explicit comparison with the Higgs/Yukawa sector.

## 8. Most promising phenomenological niche

A more defensible place to look is a **hidden sector** rather than replacing known electric charge and Standard-Model masses.

A hidden compact momentum sector could in principle produce:

- stable or metastable KK states;
- a dark `U(1)` or geometrical parity;
- radion-mediated interactions;
- pair-production thresholds;
- cosmological transitions between relativistic and nonrelativistic hidden modes.

But each of these belongs to established extra-dimensional/dark-sector phenomenology unless LTG supplies a new relation among them.

## 9. Result

### F-023E

**The first clear experimental discriminator is not the proper-time phase. It is the companion structure required by hidden momentum: tower, radion, charge relation, or KK selection rule.**

### F-023F

**The simplest model is already ruled out as a sole explanation of observed charged-lepton masses and charges.**

### F-023G

**A viable distinct LTG model must predict a new relation among companion observables rather than merely reproduce a 4D rest mass.**

## 10. Next gate

The next experiment should compare two possible endpoints:

### Endpoint A — theoretical closure
Classify LTG as a coherent reinterpretation of standard KK/null-lift/SHP-adjacent physics.

### Endpoint B — one minimal new constraint
Introduce exactly one relation among observable companion quantities—for example a relation among KK mass spacing, radion coupling, and the proper-time phase—and immediately confront it with known bounds.

No further unconstrained formal extensions should be added before this choice is made.

## Data anchor

Particle Data Group 2024/2025 summary tables give approximately

- electron mass `0.51099895 MeV`;
- muon mass `105.6583755 MeV`.

Both are charged leptons of unit electric-charge magnitude, so the minimal universal `m proportional |q|` prediction is empirically untenable for the Standard Model.
