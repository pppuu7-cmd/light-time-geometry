## 1. Four-form vacuum stress

Consider a three-form gauge potential \(A_3\) with field strength

\[
F_4=dA_3.
\]

In four dimensions the source-free equation sets the field strength to a spacetime-volume form,

\[
F_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
=
f\,
\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},
\]

where \(f\) is constant between membrane transitions.

The four-form has no ordinary local propagating wave degree of freedom in 4D.

Its stress tensor is metric proportional,

\[
T_{\mu\nu}^{(F)}
=
-\rho_F g_{\mu\nu}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\rho_F
=
\frac12 f^2>0
}
\]

in standard conventions.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
p_F=-\rho_F,
\qquad
w_F=-1.
}
\]

### Result 056A

The four-form realizes exactly the pressure-sign structure sought in EXP-045:

\[
\boxed{

\qquad

}
\]

without negative-energy particles.

## 3. Does one flux predict a tiny positive Lambda?

No.

The spacing between neighboring vacuum energies is set by

\[
q.
\]

To obtain a very small positive residual cosmological constant from a generic large bare vacuum term, one needs at least one of:

1. an extremely small charge \(q\);
2. a tuned cancellation between \(\Lambda_{\rm bare}\) and a flux contribution;
3. many independent four-form fluxes producing a dense discretuum;
4. additional membrane/selection dynamics.

Therefore one four-form does not remove continuous input data.

### Result 056C

\[
\boxed{
\text{quantization of the vacuum scale}
\neq
\text{prediction of its observed value}.
}
\]

\sum_i C_i n_i^2q_i^2,

This is the established Bousso-Polchinski mechanism / flux-landscape structure.

Hence it is not a parameter-free LTG prediction.
